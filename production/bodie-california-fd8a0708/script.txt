This California ghost town is being kept old on purpose. Welcome to Bodie, where the gold rush left behind streets, wooden buildings, and rooms still holding furniture and goods. Gold was discovered here in 1859. A later mining boom brought thousands of people, but the rush did not last. Residents moved away, and fires in 1892 and 1932 destroyed large parts of town. Mining stopped in 1942. California made Bodie a state historic park in 1962. Today, more than a hundred historic buildings remain. The preservation approach is called arrested decay: protect what survived without turning the town into a shiny reconstruction. Follow Unmapped America for the places history almost left behind.
