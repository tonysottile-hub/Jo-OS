Around 1891, gold discoveries in southeastern Nevada created a camp first called Ferguson. After mining entrepreneur John DeLamar bought the best claims in 1893, the town became Delamar and grew to more than fifteen hundred residents. It had a newspaper, hospital, school, churches, stores, saloons, and a theater. But its wealth came with a deadly cost: dry milling filled the air with fine silica dust that caused silicosis, earning Delamar the nickname "The Widow Maker." Nevada's historic marker credits the district with about twenty-five million dollars in gold before the boom faded.
