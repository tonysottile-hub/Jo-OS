Wonder, Nevada, burned bright for barely a decade. Thomas J. Stroud and fellow prospectors staked the first claims in April 1906, and by midsummer stores and saloons were open. The mining district organized that same year; a school followed in 1907. Desert life came with unusual comforts—water piped from Bench Creek, an ice plant, even a swimming pool. In 1913, the Nevada Wonder company built a 200-ton cyanide mill. State historians say its short boom produced about $6 million in silver, gold, copper, and zinc. By 1915, the boom was fading. Today, mill ruins and empty foundations mark Wonder’s spectacular rise and disappearance.
