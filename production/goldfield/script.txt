In 1902, two Nevada prospectors found gold in the desert. Within a few years, Goldfield exploded into Nevada's largest city, with roughly 20,000 people and rail connections carrying its wealth outward. The mines produced tens of millions of dollars in precious metals, and the boom transformed a remote camp into a modern city. But it didn't last. Gold production fell sharply by 1910. A flash flood hit in 1913, and in 1923 a massive fire destroyed 53 blocks of downtown. Goldfield never vanished completely. Historic buildings still stand today, preserving the remains of one of Nevada's most dramatic boomtowns.
