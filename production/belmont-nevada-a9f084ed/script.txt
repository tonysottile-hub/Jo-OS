In 1865, silver discoveries turned a high-desert spring into Belmont, Nevada. Two years later it became the Nye County seat, and at its peak the town had schools, churches, newspapers, businesses, and its surviving courthouse. Belmont produced about four million dollars in silver, but the rich ore was shallow. By 1890, most mines were no longer profitable, and residents moved on to newer strikes. The county seat left for Tonopah in 1905. Today, Belmont's courthouse and scattered ruins preserve one of Nevada's classic silver-boom stories.
