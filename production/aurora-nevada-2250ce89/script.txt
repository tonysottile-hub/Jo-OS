Aurora exploded during Nevada's early mining era and by 1863 its population approached ten thousand. The remote gold-and-silver boomtown briefly became one of the West's most important camps. A young Samuel Clemens prospected there before becoming famous as Mark Twain, and he wrote dispatches from the region. The boom did not last. Mining declined, residents left, and decades later brick salvagers stripped many of Aurora's surviving buildings. Today only foundations, mining traces, and the historic cemetery hint at the city that once stood near Nevada's western edge.
