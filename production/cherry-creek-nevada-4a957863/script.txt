Cherry Creek is the Nevada mining town that refused to vanish. In 1872, Peter Corning and John Carpenter staked the Tea Cup gold claim, and a camp rose in the mountains of White Pine County. By 1882, more than 1,800 people lived here. Then the boom broke—but Cherry Creek did not die. Gold and silver production rose and fell for decades, continuing into the 1940s. A railroad station stood four miles east, yet the town itself survived even as traffic and mining shifted away. Today, the 1872 schoolhouse and other historic buildings still stand beside a small living community. Cherry Creek is not quite a ghost town. It is a boomtown that outlived its boom.
