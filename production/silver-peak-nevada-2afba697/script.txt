Silver Peak is a Nevada town that died, burned, and came back for a completely different mineral. One of the state’s oldest mining districts had a stamp mill by 1865 and a larger one by 1867. In 1906, a new company built a railroad and a 100-stamp mill at nearby Blair. But the boom failed, and by 1917 Silver Peak was abandoned. Fire destroyed much of the town in 1948. Then the hidden wealth beneath Clayton Valley changed its fate. Lithium brine extraction began in 1966. USGS says Clayton Valley became the only lithium-brine-producing playa in the United States. Silver Peak survived because the battery age found new value beneath the desert floor.
