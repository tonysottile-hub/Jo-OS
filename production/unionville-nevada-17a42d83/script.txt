Unionville began in Buena Vista Canyon after silver discoveries in 1861. Southern sympathizers called the camp Dixie, but by 1862, neutral and Union factions had renamed it Unionville. The town became Humboldt County’s seat and boomed between 1863 and 1870, reaching about fifteen hundred people. A young Samuel Clemens briefly tried mining here—before Virginia City and before he became Mark Twain. Unionville lost the county seat to Winnemucca in 1873, and by the late 1870s most local ore was depleted. But the town never vanished completely. Fewer than two dozen residents remain among adobe ruins, stone walls, an old store, and the alleged site of Twain’s cabin—a living ghost town hidden in the canyon.
