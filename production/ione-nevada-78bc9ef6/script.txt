In 1863, silver discoveries turned Ione—pronounced “eye-own”—into a busy trade and milling center in central Nevada. When Nye County was created the next year, this town of more than 600 people became its first county seat. But richer ore at Belmont pulled miners away, and the county seat followed in 1867. Ione revived briefly with a new stamp mill in 1896, then again during a cinnabar rush in 1912. Neither boom lasted. Yet Ione never fully emptied. Through mine failures, falling silver prices, and more than a century of decline, the near-ghost town earned a stubborn nickname: “The Town That Refused to Die.”
