Midas began with a gold strike in the summer of 1907. By March 1908, rich discoveries had triggered a classic Nevada rush, and a tent town of roughly 2,000 people filled this remote canyon. The camp was first called Gold Circle, but postal officials renamed it Midas. The boom faded almost immediately: without enough stamp mills, only the richest ore could be processed, and the population collapsed to about 250 within a year. A mill built in 1909 kept the town alive until fire badly damaged it in 1922. Mining limped on until World War II—but Midas never completely disappeared, surviving today as a tiny living ghost town.
