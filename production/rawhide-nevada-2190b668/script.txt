Rawhide, Nevada, rose on equal parts gold and hype. By 1908, promoters led by George Graham Rice were selling it as the next Virginia City, and the camp swelled to about 7,000 people. But the ore never matched the headlines. Fire tore through the town that September, and a flood followed in 1909. By 1910, fewer than 500 residents remained. Modern open-pit mining later erased most of the original townsite. Rawhide didn’t simply become a ghost town—its boom was oversold, its streets were destroyed, and even its ruins were mined away.
