Metropolis, Nevada was not a mining boomtown. In 1910, the Pacific Reclamation Company planned a modern farming city surrounded by roughly forty thousand acres of farmland. Streets, sidewalks, lights, a railroad connection, a hotel, and a school followed, and hundreds of settlers arrived. But the project never secured all the water it expected. Water-rights litigation, drought, crop failures, and pests undermined the dream. The railroad eventually left, businesses closed, and by mid-century Metropolis had become a ghost town. Its school arch and hotel ruins still mark the failed desert experiment.
