In the Nevada desert, just outside Death Valley, a city appeared almost overnight. Rhyolite began after Shorty Harris and E. L. Cross found gold-bearing quartz in 1904. Within a few years, the boomtown had electricity, rail service, hotels, stores, a school, an opera house, and even a stock exchange. Then the dream cracked. The financial panic of 1907 squeezed investment. Mines closed. Banks failed. By 1911, the Montgomery Shoshone mine and mill shut down. In 1916, Rhyolite's power was switched off for good. Today, the ruins of the Cook Bank, the old depot, and a house built from thousands of glass bottles still stand in the desert. Rhyolite didn't slowly fade away. It boomed, broke, and became a ghost town in barely a decade. This is Unmapped America.
