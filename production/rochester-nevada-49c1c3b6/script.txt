Rochester Canyon once held not one Nevada boomtown, but two. Gold drew miners here in the 1860s, yet the real rush began in 1912, when Joseph Nenzel uncovered rich silver ore. A federal guide reported that more than 2,000 people crowded into the canyon within a month. Upper and Lower Rochester filled with hotels, saloons, mills—and even an orchestra. A short-line railroad arrived in 1914, then failed as an aerial tram carried ore more reliably. By 1926, the post office and most businesses were gone, though mining continued until 1942. Today, tailings bury Upper Rochester, while foundations and tram towers mark the vanished town below.
