In Candelaria, Nevada, even water told the story of the boom. Silver claims appeared in the mid-1860s, and by 1880 Candelaria was the largest town nearby—with two hotels, six stores, and ten saloons. The Northern Belle mine became its engine, producing an estimated seven million dollars, mostly in silver. But this was a brutal desert camp: water once cost a dollar a gallon. New pipelines finally cut that to five cents, yet the boom could not last. A nationwide depression halted mining in the 1890s, and the post office closed in 1935. Today, Candelaria's stone ruins are what remain of a silver town the desert slowly reclaimed.
