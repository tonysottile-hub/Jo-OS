In Nevada's Snake Range, gold discoveries in 1872 sparked the mining camp of Osceola. Placer gold followed in 1877, and by 1882 the town had grown to more than fifteen hundred people. But miners faced a stubborn problem: not enough water to wash the gold-bearing gravel. They built miles of ditches to carry mountain water to the mines, including an eighteen-mile East Ditch completed in 1890. Even that costly engineering could not guarantee profits. By 1905, mining had nearly stopped. Osceola's remains tell a story of gold, ingenuity, and the limits of a desert boom.
