Lake Mead did not just cover a canyon—it erased an entire Nevada town. St. Thomas began in 1865 as a Mormon farming settlement along the Muddy River. After an early exodus over Nevada taxes, families returned, and by 1912 the town even had a railroad stop. Then Hoover Dam began impounding the Colorado River in 1935. As Lake Mead rose, the federal government bought the land and residents left. St. Thomas disappeared beneath the reservoir. Decades later, falling water exposed its roads and foundations again—turning a drowned town into one of Nevada's strangest ghost towns.
