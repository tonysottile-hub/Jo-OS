Seven Troughs is remembered for a flood—but the truth is stranger. Gold-rich ore drew hundreds into northwestern Nevada, and by 1908 two ten-stamp mills served a camp with schools, stores, a hotel, and saloons. On July 19, 1912, a cloudburst sent a wall of water down the canyon. It demolished the cyanide plant, while nearby Mazuma took the deadliest blow; Seven Troughs itself escaped major loss. The district still produced roughly $2 million in gold between 1908 and 1918. But residents left as the boom weakened, and mining ended in the mid-1930s. Today, cabins, cars, and mill ruins remain.
