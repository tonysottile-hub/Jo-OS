High in Nevada’s White Pine Range, Treasure City rose almost overnight after rich silver discoveries on Treasure Hill in 1868. Nevada’s historical marker says the stampede drove roughly ten thousand people into huts and even caves between eight thousand and ten thousand five hundred feet. Treasure City sat beside the richest mines while Hamilton grew below. The boom even produced its own newspaper, the White Pine News, first published there in 1869. But the silver pockets faded almost as quickly as fortunes arrived. By 1875 the surrounding camps were failing. Today, scattered stone walls on the mountain mark one of the West’s shortest, fiercest silver rushes.
