A mining town can disappear while its name survives. In Lincoln County, Nevada, that name is Fay. Around the turn of the twentieth century, mining entrepreneur A. W. McCune developed the camp near the Homestake and Horseshoe mines. Businesses moved in from nearby Deerlodge, and a stage route connected Fay with the railroad at Modena, Utah. Its post office operated from September nineteen hundred until July nineteen twenty-four. Mining declined, and the community never regained its early momentum. Fay reminds us that behind a ghost-town name was a working community, built around hopes that did not last. This is Unmapped America.
